\section{The problem}

I will point a counterexample against Kakeya's conjecture for~$n=2$.

\textbf{Kakeya's conjecture for $n=2$.} A union of unit-length intervals on a plane has Hausdorff dimension~$2$, if there are intervals of all possible directions among them.

I will denote Hausdorff dimension by $\dim$.

\subsection{The counterexample}

Let $x\mapsto u(x)=(x_1,x_2,\dots,)$ be any of (many) mappings from $x\in[0;1]$ to its binary representation, i.e. $x_i\in\{0,1\}$, $u(x)=\sum_{i=1}^\infty \frac{x_i}{2^i}$.

Then define $c(x)=\sum_{i=1}^\infty \frac{2x_i}{3^i}$. It is well known that $c(x)\in C$ where $C$ is the Cantor set.

The counterexample is the union
\begin{equation}\label{maindef}
U=\bigcup_{x\in[0;1[}S_x
\end{equation}
of intervals \[ S_x = \setcond{(c(x)-t\cos(\pi x), t\sin(\pi x))}{t\in\left]0;1\right[}. \]

\begin{lem}
Each interval~$S_x$ is of length~$1$.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\sin^2(\pi x)+\cos^2(\pi x) = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}
$U$~contains intervals of all directions.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
Because $c(x)$ has all values in $\left[0;1\right[$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}
$\dim U<2$.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}

Partition $\left[0;1\right[$ into~$2^m$ intervals $I_s=\left[\frac{k}{2^m};\frac{k+1}{2^m}\right[$, $k=0,\ldots,2^m-1$, each of length $1/2^m$. Consider binary digits $s=(s_1,\ldots,s_m)$ (the first~$m$ binary digits of a binary number $\overline{0.s_1\dots s_m}$). Define $U_s=\bigcup_{x\in I_s} S_x$, so by~\eqref{maindef} we have $U=\bigcup_s U_s$.

For $x\in I_x$, express $c(x)=a_s+r$, where \[a_s=\sum_{i=1}^m \frac{2s_i}{3^i} \quad\text{and}\quad r=\sum_{i=m+1}^\infty \frac{2x_i}{3^i}.\] We have $r\leq 1/3^m$ by properties of ternary numbers.
So $c(x)\in[a_s;a_s+1/3^m]$. Approximate~$U_s$ with a parallelogram~$T_s$:
\[
T_s = \bigcup_{c\in[a_s;a_s+1/3^m]} \setcond{(c(x_s)-t\cos(\pi x_s), c(x_s)+t\sin(\pi x_s)}{t\in[0;1]},
\]
where $x_s=k/2^m$. This parallelogram has base $1/3^m$ and height $\lvert\sin(\pi x_s)\rvert$ with area $O(1/3^m)$.

For $x\in I_s$, $\lvert\cos(\pi x)-\cos(\pi x_s)\rvert$ and $\lvert\sin(\pi x)-\sin(\pi x_s)\rvert$ are (mean value theorem) $O(1/3^m)$, so $U_s$ lies within a $\delta$-neigh\-bor\-hood of~$T_s$, with $\delta\in O(1/3^m)$, because:
\begin{multline*}
\left\lvert\bigl(c(x_s)-t\cos(\pi x_s), c+t\sin(\pi x_s)\bigr) - \bigl(c(x)-t\cos(\pi x), t\sin(\pi x)\bigr)\right\rvert=\\
\left\lvert\Bigl(\bigl(c(x_s)-c(x)\bigr)-t\bigl(\cos(\pi x)-\cos(\pi x_s)\bigr), t\bigl(\sin(\pi x_s)-\sin(\pi x)\bigr)\Bigr)\right\rvert\in O(1/3^m),
\end{multline*}
because $c(x_s)-c(x)\in O(1/3^m)$ and $\lvert\cos(\pi x)-\cos(\pi x_s)\rvert$ and $\lvert\sin(\pi x)-\sin(\pi x_s)\rvert$ are $O(1/3^m)$,
$\lvert t\rvert\leq 1$.

Further, split $T_s$ into smaller parallelograms:
\[
T_{sl} = \bigcup_{c\in[a_s;a_s+1/3^m]} \setcond{\bigl(c(x_s)-t\cos(\pi x_s), c(x_s)+t\sin(\pi x_s)\bigr)}{t\in\left[\frac{l}{2^m};\frac{l+1}{2^m}\right[},
\]
where $l=0,\ldots,2^m-1$.

Let fix balls of the radius $\epsilon = 1/3^m$.

Cover $T_{sl}$ with balls.
Since $T_{sl}$ lies inside a rectangle of dimensions \( 1/3^m \times 1/2^m \), we can cover it with
\[
O(1) \cdot O(3^m / 2^m) = O(3^m / 2^m)
\]
balls.

Therefore $T_s$~can be covered by $O(2^m)\cdot O(3^m / 2^m) = O(3^m)$ and thus $U_s$ (as $O(1/3^m)$-neigh\-bor\-hood of~$T_s$) can also be covered by balls of radius $2\epsilon$ where $\epsilon\in O(3^m)$, that is by $O(3^m)$~balls of radius~$\epsilon$.

For \( U \), because it consists of \( 2^m \) sets \( U_s \), use:
\[
2^m \cdot O(3^m) = O(2^m \cdot 3^m)
\]
balls.

I remind, that $d$-di\-men\-sio\-nal Hausdorff measure is defined by the formula:
\[
H^d_{\epsilon}(U) = \setcond{(\diam U_i)^d}{\sum_{i=1}^\infty U_i\supseteq U, \diam U_i\leq\epsilon}.
\]

The $d$-di\-men\-sio\-nal Hausdorff measure sum (the sum in the above formula) can be as small as:
\[
O(2^m \cdot 3^m) \cdot O(1/3^m)^d = O(2^m\cdot 3^m\cdot 3^{-md}) = O(2^m\cdot 3^{m(1-d)}).
\]

Obviously, $2^m\cdot 3^{m(1-d)}=2^m\cdot 3^{m(-1-\epsilon)}\to 0$ for $d=2-\epsilon$ for a small $\epsilon>0$.

So the Hausdorff measure $H^d(U)=0$. Therefore \[ \dim U=\inf\setcond{d\geq 0}{H^d(U)=0}\leq 2-\epsilon. \]

\end{proof}

\begin{thm}
For Besicovitch set~$U$ its Hausdorff dimensionality is less than~$2$.
\end{thm}

Combining the lemmas.
